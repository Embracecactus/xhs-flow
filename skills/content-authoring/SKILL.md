# Content authoring

Use to clarify a single content brief or create platform-native drafts from a confirmed theme. Do not use it for publishing, data analysis, or a production pipeline that needs a specialized video skill.

## Clarify only material choices

First inspect supplied sources and project context. Confirm audience, objective, core claim, evidence, chosen platforms, format, tone, exclusions, and commercial relationship only where missing information changes the draft. State reasonable defaults and pause for direction confirmation before a full draft when the theme is genuinely undecided.

## Write from evidence

Separate verified facts, the author's judgment, inference, and advice. Do not add brands, people, metrics, results, or promises unsupported by the evidence. Use explicit placeholders for facts needing human verification.

Create multiple internal title approaches, remove those the body cannot fulfill, and present a small useful selection when title approval is needed. Design each selected platform independently: its opening, evidence order, density, examples, call to action, tags, and disclosure may differ. Shared facts are not a reason to reuse one body unchanged.

## Visual and material boundaries

Once the direction is settled by the current request or an approved brief, give a visual brief tied to each page, shot, or claim. Reuse existing decisions instead of adding another approval checkpoint. Use only user-authorized, licensed, public-domain, or generated material with documented rights; public availability alone is not reuse permission. For social-card or slide production, discover an installed compatible tool such as `guizang-social-card-skill` or `html-ppt`; they are optional external capabilities, not this skill's dependency.

Deliver the selected title, complete platform drafts, required release fields, material/rights notes, and outstanding verification items. A draft never means a platform action is authorized.

Keep quotations brief and attributed when a source requires it. Do not turn a reference style into a claim of sponsorship, endorsement, or measured result.
